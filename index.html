The problems, plainly

Structure

  • Organized by audience, not by service — so no page targets "water heater repair," "drain cleaning," "gas line," and the rest.
  • No service pages exist, so high-intent searches have nowhere to land.
  • Ranking-worthy services are buried inside segment pages instead of standing on their own.

Technical & trust

  • The site is indexed twice (http/www vs https), splitting its own authority.
  • Junk URLs (/sample/, /13010-2/, media pages) clutter the index.
  • A locale tag set to Canada (en_CA) and mixed-content warnings from the audit.
  • Reviews sit around 3.9★ while the market leader advertises 5.0 — the biggest conversion drag.
The upside hiding in this: because no service pages rank today, building them is almost pure gain. There's very little to lose and a lot of uncontested ground to take.

Our strategy

The strategy under the plan, in plain terms: what actually ranks a local plumber, why the site is being reshaped around it, and why every page is built the way it is.

1 · What actually ranks a local plumber

When someone in McMinnville searches for a plumber, Google answers with the map pack first: three business profiles, above every website result. The profile wins that spot, not the website alone. So the strategy optimizes the thing that ranks: the site exists to prove to Google that every category and service on your profile is real, deep, and local. Pages feed the profile; the profile feeds the phone.

2 · The mismatch today

Your current site is organized by who you serve: residential, commercial, industrial, wineries. Searchers do not search that way; they search for what they need, "water heater repair," "drain cleaning," "gas line installation." That mismatch is why roughly 89% of your organic traffic rides on the homepage and why zero service searches have a Gormley page to land on. The audit tab holds the evidence; the market is sitting uncontested.

3 · Why the structure looks like this

The new sitemap mirrors your Business Profile: one page per profile category, one page per service inside it, named in the searcher's language. Pages link in deliberate lanes: category pages introduce their services, service pages point back up and sideways only to their own siblings, and the links that carry this structure live inside the content, where Google gives them weight. Nothing competes with the homepage, which stays the hub and keeps every ranking it has earned.

4 · Why every page is built the same way

Every blueprint shares one spine, ordered the way a buyer actually thinks: recognize the problem, trust the company, understand the process and the cost, see proof, act. Headings alone tell each page's story, because that is how both scanners and search engines read. The service page shows the full pattern:

The spine stays; each page type then adapts it to its own buyer. That is why the blueprints are similar without being identical:

HomepageCarries the whole shop for the broadest searcher: every category introduced, the two or three highest-priority services featured, the deepest proof. It is also the landing page for the Business Profile itself.
Category pagesThe expert hub for one trade. More story and process than a service page, because this buyer is comparing companies, not booking a fix. Links down to every service it owns; the commercial ones carry the bid path.
Service pagesThe workhorse. This searcher already knows the problem, so the page leads with symptom recognition, cost honesty, and what the visit includes, then asks for the call. Shortest distance from problem to phone.
Location pagesProximity proof. Same trust spine, but the local layer does the persuading: the city's housing stock, the drive time, the calls that actually come from that town.
Vertical pagesAudience pages, not search silos. Commercial & Industrial and Wineries speak to GCs, facility managers, and winemakers: credentials forward, bid path first, service calendar second.
About · ContactTrust utilities. No keyword job; their work is the family story, the checkable credentials, and every way to reach the shop.

Two policies run underneath every section: nothing unverified ships, no invented reviews, jobs, or claims, and every review shown is a real quote from the Google profile.

5 · Why three ways in, not one form

The site deliberately runs three separate intake paths, because three different people arrive with three different transactions, and the office runs three different responses:

Schedule ServiceA homeowner's work order, opened from any page: what is wrong, how urgent, where, with an emergency interrupt that routes crises to the phone. Lands in the dispatch queue for a callback.
Request a BidA commercial transaction: company, drawings, bid due date, straight to the estimating desk with a one-business-day answer. A GC will not fill out a homeowner form, and should not have to.
ContactEverything else: vendors, paperwork, questions, applicants. A simple message box that keeps the other two queues clean.

One generic form would force the office to triage every message and force every visitor through fields that do not apply to them. Three paths mean every submission arrives with the right fields, at the right desk, with a response promise the shop can actually keep.

6 · Protected by design

Restructures fail when they move what already works. This one does not: every current URL is kept, redirected, or retired on purpose, the homepage does not move, and almost everything new is added alongside rather than in place of. The worst realistic outcome is a site with the same rankings it has today plus twenty new pages competing where you currently have none.

How you'll know it's working: map-pack presence for the category searches in the audit table, service pages earning their own rankings, and calls that reference the page someone actually read. The Migration Map next shows how we get there without risking what you have.

The build

What happens if Gormley says go. Two things stay true the whole way through: you see and approve every page before it is built, and your current website stays live and untouched until the day the new one takes its place.

Two ways to use this plan

We build it

CodeDesign produces everything on this page end to end: content, prototypes, the WordPress build, launch, and the month after. One team, one standard, priced in an itemized proposal.

Someone else builds it

This plan is complete enough to hand to another builder. If you go that way, we are available to review their build against the plan, or consult alongside it, so the strategy survives the handoff.

Everything below describes the full build with us.

See every page before it exists

Most website projects fail at the same moment: the client sees the finished site and says "that is not what I pictured." This build removes that moment. All 34 pages get finished as clickable prototypes inside this plan, exactly like the blueprints you can open today. The prototypes are medium fidelity on purpose: right structure, right sections, working copy, placeholder imagery. The finished look, final art direction, real photography, and visual polish, gets layered on during the WordPress build, on top of a structure you have already approved. You review them on any device, click a comment onto any element you want changed (we use a review tool called Feedbucket that pins your notes to the exact spot on the page), and we revise until you approve. Only approved pages get built in WordPress. Revisions are cheap in a prototype and expensive in a finished website; this order of operations keeps them cheap.

Three phases

Phase A · Content & prototypes

Ends with a complete, approved prototype of the whole site.

  • Migrate and revise current-site content per the Migration Map
  • Write the pages that don't exist yet
  • Confirm every flagged fact with you
  • Prototype all 34 pages inside this plan
  • Your comments, two revision rounds per page, sign-off

Phase B · WordPress build & launch

The approved prototype becomes the finished website, built in parallel while your current site keeps running untouched.

  • Theme built from the Style Guide and its token system; design finished to full quality
  • Every page assembled from its approved prototype
  • Scheduling, bid, and contact forms wired to your workflows
  • Schema, redirects, and the technical punch list
  • Desktop, tablet, and mobile QA, then the one-move switch with redirects live from the first minute

Phase C · Launch month

The first 30 days after the switch.

  • Real photos placed per the shot list
  • Business Profile aligned: appointment link, categories, review responses posted
  • Call and form tracking verified
  • Fixes as the site settles

How reviews work

The work happens in the open. You hold the live link for the entire project, pages appear as they are finished, and at every phase we tell you exactly which pages are ready for you and when your comments, directions, and revision requests are due, so you are never guessing when to look. Every comment you leave lands pinned to the element it is about. Each page carries two revision rounds before sign-off; changes after sign-off become change orders so the schedule holds. One decision-maker on your side keeps the rounds moving.

  • Review turnaround: comments within about two business days keeps the timeline honest
  • Responses to the two open Google reviews (drafted for you already) posted before the site links to the profile

Six to nine weeks end to end: three to four weeks for content and prototypes (the pace is mostly set by review speed), three to four weeks for the WordPress build and launch.
